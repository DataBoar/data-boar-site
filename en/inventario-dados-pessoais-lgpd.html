LGPD personal-data inventory: what it is, why it fails and how to automate it

Direct answer: a personal-data inventory is the map of which data you process, where it is and what for. Most fail because the spreadsheet does not see the real file. Automate the technical discovery on your PC (local, no cloud) and use the Excel findings as input — evidence, not a legal opinion.

Start on Windows (no Docker) →   How to find out where the data is

What the LGPD expects (in office language)

Controllers and processors need to know what they process. This shows up in the record of processing operations (Art. 37), in handling data-subject requests and, where applicable, in the RIPD (Data Protection Impact Report). Without a map, what is left is guesswork — and operational risk.

Why the spreadsheet alone is not enough

  • Interviews record what the department remembers, not the 2019 PDF in the “dead archive” folder.
  • ZIPs, ERP exports and e-mail with attachments escape the checklist.
  • The ANPD spreadsheet helps on the administrative side; it does not scan the disk.

How to automate without turning it into an IT project

  1. Validate the product with data-boar --demo (synthetic data) — Windows guide.
  2. Point only at the customers/documents folder (not the whole C:\).
  3. Read the Excel report: path + finding type (e.g. CPF). You check it and decide what to do.
  4. Complete the formal inventory (purpose, legal basis, owners) with the DPO/legal — the tool does not do that for you.

Local-first open source vs an expensive suite

If the office needs visibility and auditable evidence, without sending client documents to an inventory SaaS, the local open-core path is usually enough. Full GRC suites make sense in mature programs; for “where is the CPF?”, start light. More in OpenSource & method.
